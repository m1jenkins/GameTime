Three more serious takes.

You liked round 4’s polish but not the sun and stones. These three keep that finish and trade the landscape for sport: a running track, a timing board and a watch dial. They share the same data, controls and rules, and every control changes all three phones at once.

What carries over from round 4

The finish: one strong picture, numbers a tap away, Archivo numerals and people colors. The same encoding: position is the latest saved total divided by that person’s goal, capped at the finish. Late updates keep their place with a clock; with no update, the person waits at the start with no position.

What “serious” doesn’t add

No odds, pot size, payouts or countdown pressure on the live screen, and no ranking. The stake stays in the rules and shows up at agreement and results, as the responsible-engagement rules require. The seriousness comes from the design, not from gambling cues.

What the app already has

All three use only what each person’s row already carries: username, goal, latest saved total and when it was saved. Colors would come from the account ID on the phone. None of them needs day-by-day history.

Your call

  1. Track, Board or Dial? Mixing works too, for example the Track on the challenge screen and the Board on Home.
  2. Keep the bright people colors, or tone them down?
  3. For the Track, blue like this or a classic red?

My pick is A, the Track. It keeps round 4’s “one picture” quality, it reads as sport the moment you see it, and lanes give every friend their own space without ranking them. The Board is the safest to build and would suit Home and small widgets.
