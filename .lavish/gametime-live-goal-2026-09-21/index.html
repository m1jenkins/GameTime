01 / HOMEThe live challenge

Progress first. People next.

The poster becomes a 6.4 km readout. Three faces and their status sit directly below it. A quiet stake line leads to one action: View goal.

The progress rail is 18 pt thick. Friends stay in roster order; status never turns this into a leaderboard.

02 / GOAL & RULESThe agreement, understood

Three answers before the details.

What counts, what a miss means, and your own stake fit in one summary card. A timeline explains the dates. Full rules and a neutral exit are one tap away.

Missing activity stays unknown. A confirmed result and the simulated consequence remain separate facts.

03 / YOUA record worth keeping

The record carries the weight.

A small portrait makes room for dated challenges, shared achievements, and the distance or steps behind them. Settings move to the familiar gear.

Closed challenges stay in the record too. No streak, perfection score, or money-won total.

DESIGN SYSTEM / 10 RULES

Athletic through
precision.

Design source: your locked light athletic direction. The new composition is a proposal; current Signal implementation remains its own record.

  • 01

    One type family. SF Pro on iPhone; system sans in the browser. Heavy numerals, semibold headings, regular copy. No condensed display face or serif wordmark.

  • 02

    Scale makes the hierarchy. 104 pt hero metric, 28 pt screen title, 20 pt section title, 17 pt main copy, 13–15 pt supporting text. Tabular figures keep updates steady.

  • 03

    One accent. Ember #C44220 marks progress, the primary action, and selection. Ink #171A18, white, and mist #F3F4F1 do the rest. Color always has a text or shape cue.

  • 04

    Open space, selective cards. A 24 pt gutter and an 8 pt spacing rhythm. Home stays open; only rules and completed moments need containment.

  • 05

    Consistent geometry. 20 pt cards, 16 pt buttons, circular portraits, 18 pt progress rails. No bursts, sticker badges, or ornamental shapes.

  • 06

    Material has a job. Opaque metrics and records; a faint translucent tab bar and a light sheet shadow separate navigation. Use solid fallbacks when transparency is reduced.

  • 07

    Social stays small. Faces, names, and a status line inside an explicitly shared challenge. No public feed, follower count, ranking, or automatic invitation.

  • 08

    One dominant action. Home has View goal. Secondary choices use native rows or disclosures. All interactive targets are at least 44 pt; text reflows at larger sizes.

  • 09

    Motion confirms an event. 140 ms press response and a 220 ms sheet transition. Navigation is immediate. Proposed native haptics: soft friend update, success on a confirmed goal, one gentle near-deadline warning only with reminders enabled.

  • 10

    Be clear where it matters. Keep “simulated” beside amounts. Explain the mode in Settings and at first commitment. No persistent banner, slogans, athlete heroes, streak pressure, shared pots, or financial celebration.

INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

One challenge, from intention to record.

Each tab has a clear job. Goal details own progress and rules.

HomeThe current challenge and its next action. Any result needing review takes priority; otherwise the nearest ending active goal leads.

ChallengesActive, upcoming, invitations, and finished. Creation lives here. No public leaderboard in the default path.

YouYour private record. Settings holds Health, sharing, reminders, simulation information, support, and account controls.

Proposal boundaries

This brief introduces personal consequences only and requests portrait avatars. Existing friend-goal policies include simulated allocation, and profile photos are deferred in the current app. These mockups deliberately follow the new brief; adopting them requires a new agreement version and separate photo/privacy work. Dates, people, results, pace labels, and the shared-day grid are illustrative. The browser does not read Health, send invitations, move money, or provide native haptics.
