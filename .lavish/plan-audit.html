Section 2

Four places the plan disagrees with itself

Withdrawal is a miss, but missing proof is not a loss

The plan states both "Voluntary withdrawal after start counts as a confirmed miss" and "incomplete proof causes no loss." A withdrawal is exactly the case where proof is incomplete. The repository resolves this the other way, and D125 adds that a miss requires "explicit owner confirmation of the complete sorted nomination set" or "an explicit no-attempt acknowledgement" (DECISIONS.md:4456) — a withdrawal is neither.

Step 1 deletes the only working product

The sequence makes the new shell default and retires Personal first, then builds onboarding, the domain model, lobbies, Health adapters and the community template in steps 2–6. Between step 1 and step 6 the app has no shippable product. Fine on an unreleased branch — but the plan presents it as an ordered delivery sequence and "stop presenting those agreements" directly contradicts "keep records readable."

Three lookback windows, one of them unsatisfiable

Eligibility uses 30 days, cumulative targets use a trailing 28-day baseline, timed targets use the best run from 90 days. Nothing relates them. More concretely: requiring "at least one eligible readable sample from the previous 30 days for the selected metric" means a person who has not run in a month cannot join a running challenge — including someone starting a running challenge in order to start running.

Goal vocabulary applied to leaderboards

"Confirmed miss" and "successful participants" are goal-challenge concepts. A leaderboard has no target to miss, yet the withdrawal rule, the exclusion rule and the settlement language all use them. The figure below marks the cells this leaves undefined.

Where the settlement rules run out

Exclusion is an unpriced escape hatch

Two rules refund a participant and remove them from settlement: "unresolved technical evidence" and a "health-and-safety withdrawal… without requiring medical uploads." Both are the correct humane defaults. Both are also a free exit from a losing position, and the plan names no rate limit, no per-account cap, no disclosure to the others, and does not say whether the pool is recomputed. Since a person controls their own Health permissions, "unresolved evidence" is partly self-inducible.

Individual targets leak baseline fitness

Goal challenges may use "individual targets" derived from each person's trailing 28-day baseline, and every participant must "review the complete agreement and consent." Consenting to the complete agreement means seeing everyone's target, and a target derived from a 28-day step average is health information about that person. The plan carefully keeps raw proof private and then publishes a derivative of it.

Section 6

The Apple Health gate is the real schedule risk

Everything above is a decision. This one is an unknown, and the plan converts it into a single point of failure.

No physical-device validation has ever happened

docs/PERSONAL_HEALTH_SNAPSHOT_V2_ACCEPTANCE.md has 0 of 109 boxes checked. Its entire physical-device section is unchecked, including "Watch-written steps catch up after delayed synchronization" and "Late Watch data during the full 24-hour grace updates the open snapshot." README.md:203 states the Simulator "cannot prove Apple Health values, observer wakes, locked-device retry, or late Watch delivery."

This is true for steps — the one metric that ships. Gate 1 is not adding three metrics to a proven one. It is validating four from zero.

The plan removes the existing descope path

The current spec degrades gracefully: "If valid filtering cannot distinguish manual/imported credit, keep the mode unavailable or propose a separately named sensor-workout policy" (WEEKLY_CHALLENGES_IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md:256).

The plan replaces that with: "failure to validate any one of these sources blocks that beta milestone." Honest, but it means the least certain item gates everything else, with no fallback. Exercise minutes is the least certain — a ring total can include manually added workouts, and no production code reads appleExerciseTime today.

Worth recording as a supersession, not a UI detail: the plan's rule that a scored challenge requires "at least one eligible readable sample from the previous 30 days" reinstates a gate that D120 deliberately removed — README.md:205: "the permission request, not a positive sample, is the creation gate." Reinstating it is the right call, because HealthKit cannot report read denial and the code acknowledges this in four places. It just needs to supersede D120 explicitly.
